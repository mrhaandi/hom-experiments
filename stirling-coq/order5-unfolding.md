# Unfolding at order ≤ 5 (working notes, 2026-10-27)

Goal: Thm 37 for a single interpolation equation E : x w₁…w_k = u of order ≤ 5, with the unfolding step proved in full. Notation follows `stirling-formal.tex`. Here t = λz₁…z_k. S is a solution in lnf over C_E, and w_i = λx̄. H_i.

## 1. Shape of terms at order ≤ 5

**Lemma 1.** Let ord(A) ≤ 5.
1. Every level-2 variable of t has type o→…→o (first order). In particular it is an end node.
2. In w_i, every variable bound by a successor of an x-node has base type.

*Proof.* (1) By Lemma `levels`, a level-j variable has order at most ord(A) − 2j + 1, which is ≤ 2 for j = 2. So its arguments have order ≤ 1, i.e. they are of base type, and its successors are dummy lambdas. (2) We have x_h : C_h with ord(C_h) ≤ 3. Its arguments have order ≤ 2, so their lambdas bind variables of order ≤ 1. ∎

So the body S of t is generated by
  S ::= z_i (λȳ₁.S)…(λȳ_m.S) | y S…S | f S…S | a,
where the ȳ are first-order variables. A level-1 node is a z-node. A level-2 node is a y-node bound at a successor n.j of a z-node n.

**Lemma 2.** Let π_i be at a z_i-node n, and let the stage starting there not be final. It does not contribute iff H_i = x_j B₁…B_p for some j, i.e. the head of w_i's body is a variable. In that case the stage is π[i, i+3] and it ends at n.j.

*Proof.* π_{i+2} is at the head of H_i, and only x̄ is in scope there.
- If the head is a constant, then either the state changes (the stage contributes), or the stage is final, or ∀ wins (impossible, since t solves E).
- If the head is x_j, then π_{i+3} is at n.j and is a child of π_i (Def. 23 via the parent π_{i+1}). So the longest admissible chain is empty and the stage is π[i, i+3]. ∎

## 2. One-step unfolding as a term transformation

Let n = z_i A₁…A_m be a z-node of t with A_j = λȳ. S_j. Assume H_i = x_j B₁…B_p (Lemma 2). Let ny = y_l R₁…R_s be an occurrence in S_j with y_l bound at n.j. Define

  t′ = t[ ny := z_i A₁ … A_{j−1} (λȳ′. y′_l R̄′) A_{j+1} … A_m ],

where:
- the A_h (h ≠ j) are copies, shifted past the binders between n and ny;
- R̄′ is R̄ with the variables bound at n.j rebound to the new λȳ′.

This is Def. 47 with k = 2. "S3 unfolded into S6" in the paper's Example 14 is exactly this case: w₁ = λy₁y₂. y₂ y₁ y₁, with x_j = y₂.

**Linearity condition (L).** x_j does not occur in B₁…B_p, i.e. the head variable of w_i is not used again inside w_i.

**Theorem 3.** Under (L), t w̄ and t′ w̄ have a common β-reduct. Hence, by confluence, t′ solves E iff t solves E.

*Proof.*
1. Reduce both terms to S{z̄:=w̄}, then contract the redex w_i Ā at n:
   w_i Ā →* A_j B̄{x̄:=Ā} →* S_j{ȳ := B̄{x̄:=Ā}}.
   By (L), S_j occurs exactly once in the result.
2. At the position of ny, the term obtained from t holds B_l{x̄:=Ā} R̄{ȳ:=B̄{x̄:=Ā}}.
3. The term obtained from t′ holds w_i Ā″ there, where Ā″ agrees with Ā off j. Contracting it gives
   w_i Ā″ →* (λȳ′. y′_l R̄′) B̄{x̄:=Ā″} →* B_l{x̄:=Ā″} R̄{ȳ:=B̄{x̄:=Ā″}},
   and B̄{x̄:=Ā″} = B̄{x̄:=Ā} by (L).
4. Nothing else differs. ∎

Remarks:
- The proof uses no game axioms; it needs only β-reduction, substitution and confluence.
- Neither stage has to be non-contributing; (L) alone suffices.
- Iterating inside the copy gives the enlarged combs (c₁…c_k) of Def. 47 that start at a z-node with a linear head.

## 3. The non-linear case: where Gap 4 really is

If x_j occurs again inside w_i, then S_j is entered several times, with different bindings of ȳ. An example is w₂ = λy₃. y₃ (y₃ a λw₁w₂.w₁)(λv₁v₂. g v₂) in Example 12. One copy placed at ny cannot reproduce all of these visits, so no single-copy unfolding is a β-equivalence.

Def. 47 does not produce a term in this situation, already for k = 2 (when the excursion of N₂ is not absorbed by a recurrent chain):
- Let N₁ be the stage at n, ending at n.j (via the head x_j). Let N₂ be a stage at ny whose B_l has head x_j (a re-entry). Then N₂ also ends at n.j, i.e. N₂+1 → N₁ at the leaf n.j.
- In the enlarged comb (c₁c₂), the copy of the leaf n.j already carries c₂. The redirected stage N₂+1 must hang under the same copy.
- So the copy of n.j gets two different subtrees and the result is not a term.

In the linear case this cannot happen: only N₁ enters n.j, because x_j occurs only at the head.

The paper's argument also has a per-play/per-visit problem, Gap 4(iii) in the notes: two visits of the same node can get different enlarged combs.

### 3a. What the non-linear case computes

With H_i = x_j B̄ and x_j free in B̄:
- N = z_i Ā ≡ S_j{ȳ := B̄{x̄:=Ā}}, and B̄{x̄:=Ā} contains A_j again.
- So S_j has *instances*:
  - the head instance, where ȳ ↦ B̄{Ā};
  - one instance for each x_j-occurrence x_j C̄ inside B̄ that the evaluation reaches, where ȳ ↦ C̄{Ā, v̄:=…}.
- The instances form a tree whose depth is at most the nesting depth of x_j in w_i. That depth is a constant of E, but the number of instances is not bounded.

A one-copy unfolding at ny agrees with the head instance and disagrees with the others. Two directions remain open:
1. **Game-relative unfolding.** Show that in a smallest solution the plays that reach ny through a re-entry instance can be dealt with first, e.g. that they never reach ny, or reach it with the same continuation. This is a statement about plays and needs the G/U layer.
2. **Separating head and re-entry.** Transform E so that the head occurrence and the inner occurrences of x_j become different parameters, and transfer small solutions back. The naive version (w_i′ = λx̄x′. x_j B̄[x′/x_j]) loses the constraint that both copies of A_j coincide.

## 6. Unfolding followed by T2, as one step (2026-10-27)

For k = 2 with a linear head, unfolding followed by T2 on the enlarged comb replaces y_l R̄ at ny by the continuation of stage N₂. That continuation is R_s if N₂ returns through v_s, or the subtree under n.h if N₂ returns through x_h and that subtree does not use ȳ_h.

This direct cut never applies to a **smallest** solution t, because it would shrink t. So the proof of Thm 37 does not get its small term by cutting t. It gets it as follows:
- The partition tree unrolls t along the plays: every visit (stage) has its own comb.
- Unfolding and T2 act on this unrolled tree, per visit.
- The small term is reassembled from the result. Separators make sure that one leaf receives the same subterm from every edge entering it.

The non-linear obstruction sits exactly at this level. In the enlarged comb (c₁c₂), the copy of the leaf n.j is entered twice by the same play:
- once from the head x_j, at the end of N₁, where it must lead to c₂;
- once through a re-entry x_j inside B_l, at the end of N₂, where it must lead to the continuation, i.e. the root comb of S_j.

Both entries go to the same λȳ′, so the two visits cannot get different subtrees. A fix has to change what the re-entry jumps to. One idea is a second copy of z_i for the re-entry; another is to unfold N₂ further before attaching the continuation. Only a change of that kind makes the two visits distinguishable.

## 7. Example (2026-11-02): re-entry absorbed by recurrence

E: X w = g (g a), with w = λx. x (λv. x (λv'. v)) (non-linear head) and t = λz. z (λy. g (y a)).

The single play visits the y-node twice. Stages:
- S1 = π[1,2];
- S2 = π[3,6], the z-stage, non-contributing;
- S3 = π[7,8], the stage at g;
- S4 = π[9,18], the y-stage: the chain π11, π16 has the recurrence property, since π[12,15] corresponds to π[6,9], so the re-entry lies inside S4;
- S5 = π[19,20], final.

S4 contributes, so Def. 47 does not apply here. The obstruction of §3 needs a re-entry excursion that is **not** complementary to an earlier interval and a stage that does not contribute. No such example is known yet; it may be impossible. The naive term-level unfolding is still unsound here: t′ w = g a.

## 8. Example where the obstruction really occurs (2026-11-02)

E: X w = f (f a d) d, with w = λx. x (λv. x (λv'. v)) as in §7 and t = λz. z (λy. f (y a) d). Here f : (0,0,0) and a, d : 0.

t solves E: S{y := K_v} = f v d, hence y a ↦ f a d and t w = f (f a d) d.

Nodes:
- t: (1)λz, (2)z, (3)λy, (4)f, (5)λ, (6)y, (7)λ, (8)a, (9)λ, (10)d;
- w: (11)λx, (12)x, (13)λv, (14)x, (15)λv', (16)v.

Play B: ∀ chooses branch 1 at the root f and branch 2 at the re-entry.
```
1 (0)  2 (1)  3 (2)z  4 (11)  5 (12)x  6 (3)  7 (4)f[f(fad)d]  8 (5)[fad]
9 (6)y  10 (13)  11 (14)x  12 (3)  13 (4)f[fad]  14 (9)[d]  15 (10)d  16 (10)[∃]
```
Stages of play B:
- S1 = π[1,2];
- S2 = π[3,6], the z-stage, ending at (3);
- S3 = π[7,8], f, contributes;
- **S4 = π[9,12], y.** The chain from π11 is empty: in this play nothing returns to a child of π11. So p = 0 and S4 ends at (3) = n.1 through the re-entry. It does not contribute.
- S5 = π[13,14], f, contributes; S5 → S2;
- S6 = π[15,16], final.

S2 unfolded into S4 satisfies Def. 47 (a)–(d):
- (a) same family, no contribution;
- (b) distinct combs (2) and (6), distinct paths (11)(12) and (13)(14);
- (c) S3 → S2 and S5 → S2;
- (d) (13) is a successor of (12).

In (c₁c₂), the copy of (3) carries the copy of (6). Redirecting S5 puts the comb (4) under the same copy of (3), so the result is not a term.

In play A (branch 1 at the re-entry) the same y-stage absorbs the re-entry through recurrence and contributes, as in §7. So whether the obstruction occurs depends on the play.

## 9. Proposed fix: a new definition of stages (2026-11-02)

The fix changes **Def. 43** (stages), specifically when a stage absorbs a re-entry. It follows LMCS 2009, Def. 8.13. It does not change Def. 47.

**Why the old definition fails.**
- The chain of Def. 21–23 requires π(j\_2m) to be a *child* of π(j\_{2m−1}).
- In play B (§8) the re-entry into (3) is followed by f, where ∀ chooses another branch. π14 is not a child of the x at π11, so the chain is empty and S4 ends at (3).
- Complementarity has a clause (b) for divergence at a constant, but a chain never gets to use it.

**Definition 43′ (stage at a variable node).** Let the stage start at π_i, at a variable node n, and let π_{i+1} be the jump into some w_q. Follow the play. The stage ends at the first position π_j that satisfies one of the following.
- **(E1)** π_j is a child of π_i: a λ-node, successor of n, with the table of π_i. This is the ordinary return.
- **(E2)** π_j is a jump from a variable node of w_q into a λ-node m of t, and no position before π_j is at m. This is a *new* entry.
- **(E3)** The play leaves a skipped corresponding interval (see below) because ∀ chooses a different branch at a constant. Then π_j is the λ-position right after that choice, a leaf of the constant's comb.
- **(F)** π_j is final.

If π_j is a jump into a λ-node m of t that *was* visited earlier, at π_{j′} say, the stage does not end. Instead it skips the maximal interval π[j, j+h] that corresponds to π[j′, j′+h] (Def. 32), and then continues with the same rules. Correspondence may break at a jump (the tables differ) or at a ∀-choice (E3).

Constant stages and stage 1 are as before.

**Play B recomputed.**
- S1 = π[1,2]; S2 = π[3,6] (z, ends at (3)); S3 = π[7,8] (f).
- **S4 = π[9,14] (y).**
  - π12 is a re-entry into (3), which was visited at π6.
  - π[12,13] = (3)(4) corresponds to π[6,7]. ∀ then chooses branch 2 where it chose 1 before.
  - By (E3) S4 ends at π14 = (9), a leaf of the comb (4).
  - S4 contributes, since [fad] becomes [d].
- S5 = π[15,16], d, final. Its edge goes to the comb (4) at the leaf (9), where the play actually is.

Def. 47(a) now forbids unfolding S2 into S4, so nothing has to be attached under the copy of (3).

**Play A** (branch 1 at the re-entry): π[12,15] corresponds to π[6,9]. Correspondence breaks at the jump π16 = (15) versus π10 = (13). The stage continues and ends at π18 = (7) by (E1). This is the same S4 = π[9,18] as before; it contributes.

**Lemma (sketch, not proved).** In an unfolding (N₁…N_k) built from stages of Def. 43′, the copy of a leaf receives at most one subtree.

*Argument.* For a conflict, some N_i must end, without contributing, at a leaf L of some c_{i′} under which a comb is already attached.
- (E3) always contributes: a ∀-choice at a constant of positive arity changes the state.
- (F) is the last stage, allowed by Def. 47(c).
- (E1) ends at N_i's own leaf, where nothing has been attached yet.
- (E2) ends at a node m visited for the first time. But a comb is attached under the copy of L only if an earlier N_{i″} ended at L, so L was visited before. Hence (E2) cannot end at an occupied leaf either.

**What it costs.**
1. A stage can end at a leaf of a foreign comb, as S4 does at (9). Def. 46 must attach the next stage where the play actually is, like the edges ←π in LMCS.
2. Uniqueness of the partition (Fact 33) must be proved again for Def. 43′.
3. Prop. 34 changes: (2) and (3) talk about children of π_i, and (E2)/(E3) endings are new.
4. Fact 36 must be re-checked: the play of (N₁…N_j) has to skip the same corresponding intervals.
5. The count of contributing stages does not get worse, because one stage may now contain several changes of the right term.
6. Linear heads are affected too: a return through x_h to an n.h that was visited before is now absorbed instead of ending the stage.

## 10. LMCS strategy, level 3: exclusion from unfolding (2026-11-09)

This section uses the **old** Def. 43 for stages, without the fix of §9. Alx asked to try Stirling's third mechanism from LMCS 2009 (Def. 9.9, Fact 9.10, proof of Thm 9.11).

### 10.1 Translation into the old paper

- **Tree.** The partition tree has one node per stage (Def. 46). Its edge N′ → leaf L of comb c says that stage N′ starts under L.
- **Extraction.** Hang every stage under the leaf its edge points to. Identify stages hanging under one leaf, then close under "children of identified stages hanging under the same leaf are identified". This is ≅ of LMCS Def. 9.9.
- **Subterm property.** Identified stages carry the same comb. If this holds, the extraction is a term (Fact 9.10).
- **Exclusion.** Run the unfoldings (Def. 47). Suppose the result fails the subterm property because of two tiles τ_k, τ_l with the same comb and an edge to the same leaf, one of which lies in an unfolded part. Then forbid unfolding of the stage they both depend on, and run the whole sequence again. Each exclusion leaves one more stage that T2 cannot remove, so it costs one term in the bound.

### 10.2 The example of §8

Before unfolding, play B has these edges:
- S3 = f(4) and S5 = f(4) both hang under the leaf (3) of S2 = z(2);
- S4 = y(6) hangs under (5) of S3;
- S6 = d(10) hangs under (9) of S5.

S3 ≅ S5, and their children are compatible: (5) → y, (9) → d. So the extraction gives t back, and the subterm property holds.

Unfolding S2 at S4 replaces S4 by z(λy′. y′ □). S5 must now hang under the copy λy′, which already carries y′. The subterm property fails.

In Stirling's diagnosis, τ_k and τ_l are S3 and S5: the same comb f(4) with edges to the same leaf (3), S5 lying in the unfolded part. Both depend on S2. **Exclusion forbids unfolding S2.** Nothing else is unfoldable. The extraction is t, which solves E. So the strategy works on the example, though trivially.

### 10.3 Exclusion is not needed here if (a) is read over the tree

Def. 47(a) asks that the stages do not contribute. In §8 this was checked in play B alone. But plays A and B coincide up to π11, so S4 is **one node** of the partition tree for both plays, and S4 contributes in play A.

If (a) is read as in LMCS Def. 9.1 and 9.5(3), "non-contributing in every play through this node", then S2 is not unfoldable and the conflict never arises. This needs no change to Def. 43.

**Claim.** At order ≤ 5, with the old Def. 43: if N₂ ends at a re-entry into n.j without contributing in some play B, then N₂ contributes in some play A that passes the same node of the partition tree. So the tree-wide (a) blocks every unfolding that could lead to the conflict of §3/§8.

*Argument (sketch; it relies on the absorption by a recurrent chain working as in §7).*
1. Let π_e be the re-entry into n.j in B, i.e. the jump from the inner x_j (at position π_x) into n.j. Let π_s be the earlier visit of n.j (the end of N₁), after which the play reached the node of N₂.
2. The re-entry instance has the same bindings as the earlier visit for every variable except ȳ: free variables of S_j other than ȳ are z's, or ȳ′ of ancestors of n, and the re-entry comes in through the closure of A_j. So the instance copies the earlier play from π_s until (i) a ∀-choice at a constant that differs, or (ii) a ȳ-node.
3. In case (ii) the ȳ-node jumps into some C_m, which is a child of π_x. The interval from π_e copies the one from π_s, which gives the recurrence property, so the chain is non-empty and N₂ does not end at the re-entry. Since B's N₂ does end there, B's first departure is of kind (i), at a constant c of arity > 0, and no ȳ-node comes before c.
4. Let A be the play equal to B up to c, which then copies the earlier choices. Such a play exists because the head constant at each node agrees with the state (t solves E), so the same branch index is available. A copies the earlier play up to the node of N₂, which is a ȳ-node. So A gets a recurrent chain, N₂ is absorbed as in §7, and N₂ passes c. Hence N₂ contributes in A.
5. A and B share N₂'s node, because they coincide up to c and c comes after the start of N₂.

So the "different binding of ȳ" case cannot end a stage at a re-entry: reaching a ȳ-node creates the chain. At order ≤ 5 the conflict comes only from ∀-choices, and these are always caught by a play A.

### 10.4 The bound: where Stirling's count is unclear

Stirling bounds the excluded tiles by (δ+p)·N(n), as "x-special or dependents of x-special tiles". In §10.2 the excluded tile S2 is neither of these. It is the **binding ancestor** (family root) of tiles that hang under it.

A count that does work, provided every excluded tile is a family ancestor of an x-special tile, is the following. Each tile has at most p (≤ 2 at order 5) family ancestors, so there are at most p·(δ+p) excluded tiles. This proviso is exactly what LMCS does not prove ("Both these tiles are dependents of the same tile in T_j").

### 10.5 Consequence and next steps

If the Claim holds, then at order ≤ 5 neither Def. 43′ (§9) nor exclusion (§10.1) is needed. It is enough to read Def. 47(a) over the tree of all plays: a stage counts as contributing if it contributes in some play passing its node. This matches LMCS Def. 9.1(1) and 9.5(3).

What this reading costs:
1. The counting must use the tree-wide notion. Contributing nodes are still at most |u| per play, and at most |u|·(number of plays) ≤ |u|² overall, so the bound gets worse only polynomially.
2. Point 3 of the argument needs the precise old definition of chains and recurrence (Defs. 21–23, 32) to be checked against "the interval copies the earlier one". So far this is shown only on §7.
3. Fact 36 and the separators must be re-checked with the tree-wide (a).
